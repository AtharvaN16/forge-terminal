Video and sound · round 2

Video and sound get one sheet: every setting on one screen, the file now on the left and what it will be on the right, one encode at the end. The case it’s built around: a 184 MB screen recording that has to go into Slack or a GitHub comment, with the dead first seconds cut off. Save as and the size field are captured from the app at 80 × 24; everything else is drawn with the app’s own art functions.

WHEN IT REFUSES

  1. The budget. Before anything is encoded: size × 8 ÷ length gives the bits a second can spend. The sound takes its share first (64 kbps, down to 32 kbps mono at the bottom).
  2. The ladder. The picture steps down until each pixel gets enough bits: full size, then the long side at 1920, 1280, 854; the frame rate the source’s, then 30, 24, 15. The first rung that fits wins, and the sheet shows it (S2).
  3. The floor. The bottom rung (854 wide, 15 fps, mono sound) at the fewest bits per pixel that still reads. About 0.02 for a screen recording; I’d set the number by measuring real recordings, not pick it here. For this 2:10 recording that is about 2.5 MB; for a one-hour talk about 70 MB, so 10 MB is never offered for it.
  4. Below the floor. The Size steps don’t go there (Z1). Typed, it is refused on the spot (Z2) and the CLI says the same. So you never wait two minutes to be told no.
  5. Above the file. 25 MB asked of a 20 MB video: it already fits, so nothing is re-encoded and Done says so. Never a bigger file (as lossy PNG does).
  6. A near miss. Rare with two passes. If it lands over, one more pass at 95% of the budget; still over, today’s “can’t get below” screen with “Use 10.3 MB anyway”.

RULES · awkward files

  1. Probed by content. Box signatures (ftyp, EBML, RIFF, OggS, fLaC, ID3, FORM) say video or sound without ffmpeg; ffprobe then gives the streams, length, size and rotation. An animated GIF dropped in can go to MP4 (often 20× smaller).
  2. Copy when nothing needs re-encoding. H.264 in MOV → MP4, Length, mute and sound only (AAC → M4A) copy the streams. The right card says copy or encode before you save.
  3. Streams, never buffers. ffmpeg writes a temp file beside the output, then it is renamed (invariant 6). Free space is checked first.
  4. Rotation, odd sizes, frame rates that vary, HDR. Upright output; sizes rounded to even; variable frame rate kept; HDR tone-mapped for H.264, WebM and GIF, kept by HEVC.
  5. Tracks, subtitles, chapters, metadata. Kept where the target can carry them; GPS stripped as for images.
  6. Several files. The sheet sets them all; Length is faint (one file only); one at a time, “2 of 5” above the bar.
  7. Long jobs. The Mac kept awake while it encodes, and only then.
  8. Encoders. No size set: Apple’s hardware encoder (fast). A size set: x264 (smaller for the same quality). Windows: x264 throughout.

BEYOND CLOP

  1. One sheet, one encode. Clop runs a fixed preset per drop; trimming, muting and fitting 10 MB in one go is not possible there.
  2. You see the result before it is made. The right card shows the picture, frame rate and size it will come out at, and whether it is a copy.
  3. Refused instantly, not after a long encode. The floor is arithmetic, so impossible sizes are never offered.
  4. Copy, don’t re-encode, when possible. Clop always re-encodes.
  5. Length, with the sound. Clop has no trim at all.
  6. HDR that doesn’t wash out, a bar that divides by the output’s length (compressO’s stalls at 50% on a 2× speed-up), loudness in two passes, sound sizes before encoding.
  7. Smaller download. Clop ships 66 MB of tools; compressO 124 MB of ffmpeg.
