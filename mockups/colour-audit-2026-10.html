The verdict

Built feature by feature, not around the job. Each round added a capability where it fit (a tab, a window, a key, a chip). The mode now holds about ten tools, and none of them leads.
No main path. Every screen shows every option at once, all at the same weight. Nothing says “do this first” or “you’re done”.
No shared key grammar. Each of the three tools uses its own keys, so what you learn on one screen doesn’t help on the next.
The output is invisible. The real deliverable, a card you paste into Figma, is hidden behind “copy all”.

All findings

1

The three cards don’t say which job they do

Two of the three subtitles end “from one colour”. Someone holding a brand hex can’t tell Scale from Palette. Someone who wants “a palette” finds two cards that make one: Extract (from a photo) and Palette (from a colour). The names describe what the code does, not what the user has or wants.

Critical · Match the real world
Reopens a decision: C1 three cards (2026-10-13)
2

Nothing says what the result is for, or when you’re done

Every screen ends in c copy all. What it copies is an SVG card that only makes sense pasted into Figma; pasted into Notes or VS Code it’s a wall of markup. The word “Figma” appears nowhere. ↵ copy copies the hex only, while the screen also shows rgb() and oklch(). There’s no finish line: people don’t know they’ve got what they came for.

Critical · Visibility of system status
3

Ten tools behind three cards

Count what’s in the mode: Extract, go deeper, Scale, Neutral, Alpha, Mix, make centre, Tweak (4 sliders), Steps (3 sets, up to 4 values), Palette, and 7 palette modes. Mix has no card at all; the only way in is the ⇧ → chip in the field. A first-time user meets alpha, oklch and contrast ratios before they’ve got one plain result. Nothing is ranked: every tool is on screen at the same weight.

Critical · Progressive disclosure
4

The same key means different things on different screens

There’s no shared key grammar, so nothing learnt on one screen carries to the next. s is “scale” on Extract but “steps” everywhere else. r is “make centre” on Scale but “reset” on Palette and in Tweak. ←→ does five different jobs. A S D are keyboard positions, not letters, so only S matches its card. See the key map below.

Critical · Consistency and standards
5

Key hints are spread over four rows

On Scale, keys are taught in four places: ⇧ → in the field, tab switch on the tab row, t tweak · s steps under the ramp, and six more in the key row. Everywhere else in Forge the key row is where you look; here it’s missing four of the keys, so the eye has to hunt.

Major · Recognition over recall
6

A wall of jargon

One Scale screen shows oklch(63% 0.196 273), 3.6:1, normal / large, AA / AAA, Tailwind 50 – 950. The other tabs add #0021FC9A, “60% opaque”, “over white” and “soft tint”. Mix adds “OKLab straight across”, Steps adds “Tailwind / Even / Radix”, and the modes are Analogous, Split complementary, Triadic. Designers know some of these words; most people know none.

7

What you came for isn’t the biggest thing on screen

On Scale, the preview block and contrast table weigh more than the ramp, which is the actual result. The chosen step is marked only by a thin ▔ under it. On Palette, “your colour” is a tiny unexplained corner mark (◤ or ▖).

Major · Visual hierarchy
8

Three tools, three layouts, three ways to choose

Extract is a vertical list, marked with a ▌ bar, moved with ↑↓. Scale and Mix are a horizontal ramp, marked with a ▔ underline, moved with ←→. Palette is a grid with a framed cell, moved with ↑↓←→. Learning one doesn’t help with the next.

9

The Tweak window covers what it changes

Tweak opens in the middle, over the ramp or grid. You move brightness while the swatches you’re changing are hidden; the only feedback is a thin chip strip inside the window. The Mode list covers the grid the same way, though its chips do help.

Palette hides half its rows

At 80×26 you see red to teal: 5 of 9 rows. Blue, purple, pink and neutral sit below, and the only sign of them is a thin scrollbar. The mode name (“Full set”) sits alone at the bottom left with no label, so it reads like a heading for nothing.

Dead ends between the tools

Extract → Scale (s) and Scale → Mix (⇧→) exist. Palette → one row’s scale, Scale → Palette, and an extracted colour → Palette don’t. esc on Palette drops you on colour’s home and the colour is gone. The obvious chain, “photo → pick the brand colour → build a palette”, can’t be done.

What the redesign should do

Principles to carry into the mockups. Not mockups yet.

  1. Start from what the user has. A photo or a colour: two ways in, not three tools. A colour leads to one result first, then grows (“a scale → the rest of the palette”), instead of two cards that both say “from one colour”.
  2. One plain result first, everything else on request. Neutral, Alpha, Mix, Tweak, Steps and Modes move into one “more” place, opened by one key, instead of sitting across four rows of every screen.
  3. Name the way out. The copy actions say where the colours go (e.g. Figma, or a hex). Any new words are listed for your approval first.
  4. One key grammar across colour mode. Arrows always move the selection, ↵ always copies the chosen colour, one letter for each verb everywhere, and every key lives in the key row.
  5. One layout family and one selection mark. A palette is ramps stacked; an extract is a list of the same swatches. The chosen colour is marked the same way on every screen.
  6. Words before numbers. oklch, contrast ratios and alpha hex become details you open, not the first thing you read.
  7. Tools hand off to each other. From any colour you can go to its scale or its palette without typing it again.
